Controls and rules

Switch pumps, pipes and valves during a run: simple controls, rules, and ready-made samples.

Controls change a pump, a pipe or a valve while an extended-period run goes on: a pump that fills a tank and stops when it is full, a main closed for a repair, a valve that lowers night pressure. FlowCHAOS has two kinds. Simple controls have one trigger and one action each. Rules combine several conditions with AND and OR, have an ELSE, and a priority that settles conflicts.

Both editors offer samples written with your network's own pumps, tanks, valves and pipes, so a sample runs as soon as it is added and you only change the numbers.

Simple controls

Analysis > Scheduled Pipe Controls lists one control per row:

Column Meaning
Link The pump, pipe or valve the control changes
Set to OPEN, CLOSED, or a number: the relative speed of a pump (1.0 is full speed) or the setting of a valve (pressure in m for a pressure valve, flow for a flow valve)
Trigger At elapsed time, At clock time, When node rises above, When node falls below
Time For a time trigger: hours into the run (6 or 6:30); for a clock time: 6 AM, 10:30 PM or 22:00
Node and Level or pressure For a level trigger: a tank (its water level above its bottom) or a junction (its pressure), and the value in metres

The box under the table reads the selected control as a sentence, for example "Open PU1 every day at 10 PM. Runs in the advanced engine.", and turns red with the reason when a control cannot run (a pipe given a speed, a node that is not in the network, a time with 75 minutes). The row number shows the same warning. OK refuses a control that cannot run and says why.

Start from a sample offers, depending on what the network has:

  • Fill the tank: pump on when low, off when full: the pump starts below a low level and stops above a high level, taken from the tank's own minimum and maximum levels.
  • Night-time pumping on the cheap tariff: the pump runs from 10 PM to 6 AM.
  • Slower pump in the day, full speed at night: 80 % speed from 6 AM, full speed from 10 PM.
  • Pipe shut for a repair from hour 8 to hour 12: who loses water during a repair.
  • Open a bypass when pressure drops: a pipe opens below 15 m at the highest junction and closes again above 25 m.
  • Lower the valve setting at night: less pressure, and less leakage, from 11 PM to 5 AM.
  • Close the tank inlet before it overflows.

Imported controls in other forms, or naming an element that is no longer in the network, are kept unchanged and listed under the table.

Elapsed-time controls that open or close run in every solver; clock times, speeds, settings and level or pressure triggers run in the advanced engine, which is the default for extended-period runs.

Rules

Analysis > Rule-Based Controls edits the rules as text, one clause a line:

RULE PumpOn
IF TANK T1 LEVEL BELOW 1.5
THEN PUMP PU1 STATUS IS OPEN
PRIORITY 2
Clause Form
RULE RULE and a name, one word
IF object id attribute relation value
AND, OR more conditions (AND also adds actions after THEN)
THEN PUMP, PIPE, VALVE or LINK id STATUS or SETTING IS value
ELSE the action when the conditions are false
PRIORITY a number; when two rules disagree, the higher priority wins
  • Objects: JUNCTION, TANK, RESERVOIR, NODE, PIPE, PUMP, VALVE, LINK and SYSTEM.
  • Node attributes: PRESSURE, HEAD, LEVEL, DEMAND, FILLTIME and DRAINTIME (hours to fill or drain a tank).
  • Link attributes: FLOW, STATUS, SETTING and POWER.
  • System: TIME (hours into the run), CLOCKTIME (10 PM, 06:00) and DEMAND (total demand).
  • Relations: =, <>, <, >, <=, >=, IS, NOT, ABOVE and BELOW.
  • A semicolon starts a comment.

The editor colours keywords, objects, attributes, relations and numbers. The samples on the left, for this network:

  • Pump follows the tank level: two rules keep the tank between a low and a high level.
  • Night tariff with a full-tank guard: the pump fills the tank at night while it has room, and rests in the day unless the tank is nearly empty; an emergency rule with a higher priority starts it whenever the level is very low.
  • Pump speed follows the town's demand: full speed above 120 % of the average demand, 75 % otherwise, as a variable speed drive does.
  • Duty and standby pumps: the second pump starts only when the first cannot keep up.
  • Pressure management at the critical point: a pressure valve raises its setting when the highest junction falls below 15 m.
  • Burst drill: isolate a main for four hours.
  • Tank overflow protection: the inlet shuts when the tank is nearly full, with the highest priority.
  • Fire reserve: the pump starts when the level is low or the tank would drain within two hours.

Add to Rules appends the selected sample (a double-click does the same; rules that would share a name are renamed), Replace Rules starts again from the sample alone.

Check reads every clause against the network: unknown keywords, objects and attributes, names that are not in the network, a pump called a pipe, a pipe given a setting, a rule without a THEN, two rules with the same name. Each problem is listed with its line; click it to go to the line. OK checks too and asks before keeping rules with problems.

Rules run in the advanced engine.

Tips

  • Give a pump that a rule controls a starting status in its properties; rules change it from the first time step on.
  • Use a gap between the start and stop levels of a tank (at least half a metre) so the pump does not start and stop at every step.
  • Watch the result with Show on Map and the time bar, or plot the tank level and the pump flow under Analysis > Advanced Graphs.
