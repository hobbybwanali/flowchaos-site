User guide

The network model

The elements of a network, the data each one carries, and the physics FlowCHAOS applies to them.

Physical elements

Junctions

A junction is a point where pipes meet and where water can be taken out (or put in, as a negative withdrawal). Its data:

Property Meaning
Ground elevation the level of the ground at the junction, from a DEM, online terrain or typed in
Design offset how far the pipe lies below (negative) or above the ground; the hydraulic elevation is ground plus offset
Design withdrawal the average flow taken at the junction (L/s), the base demand
Demand profile multipliers of the withdrawal through the day; none means the project's default profile
Demand categories several withdrawals at one junction, each with its own profile (houses, a school, a leak), as imported models give them; FlowCHAOS keeps and analyses them
Outflow coefficient an emitter: an outflow that grows with pressure, q = C p^n, for leaks, sprinklers and hydrants
Starting concentration the water quality at the start of a run
Injection a water quality source here (see Water quality)

The analysis gives each junction its head, its pressure (head minus hydraulic elevation), the withdrawal actually delivered, and its water quality.

Reservoirs

A reservoir is a source or sink of unlimited capacity whose water level does not change with the flow: a lake, a river, a groundwater aquifer at its pumping level, or a connection to a bulk supply.

Property Meaning
Total head the level of the water surface (m above datum)
Water level above ground another way to set it: the head follows the ground level when it changes
Head profile multipliers that make the level vary in time (a tide, a river that rises and falls)
Starting concentration, injection the quality of the water it supplies

A borehole is modelled as a reservoir at its pumping water level (static level plus drawdown), with the submersible pump as a pump link from it.

Tanks

A tank stores water; its level rises when more flows in than out and falls otherwise.

Property Meaning
Ground elevation and stand height the bottom of the tank is the ground plus the stand (for an elevated tank)
Diameter of a cylindrical tank
Minimum, maximum and starting levels the water depth limits and the depth at the start of the run
Minimum volume water below the minimum level that is never used
Shape curve volume against depth for tanks that are not cylinders; the diameter is then ignored
Spills when full extra inflow is lost over the top, rather than the inlet being closed
Mixing behaviour, inlet zone fraction, decay rate in tank how water mixes and reacts inside it (see Water quality)

When a tank reaches its maximum level its inflow stops (unless it spills); when it reaches its minimum level its outflow stops.

Pipes

A pipe carries water from its start node to its end node; the flow is negative when water runs the other way.

Property Meaning
Length taken from the drawing, or a model length when the drawing is schematic
Diameter internal diameter in mm
Roughness the friction coefficient of the chosen head loss formula
Fittings loss factor K the minor losses of bends, tees and valves, as a sum of their K values
Starting status open, closed, or a check valve (water flows one way only)
Material used by quantities, wall decay by material and pipe series
Bulk and wall decay rates this pipe's own reaction rates; empty means the project's

Pumps

A pump adds head to the water flowing through it, from its start (suction) to its end (delivery).

Property Meaning
Head-flow curve the pump's curve from the Performance Curve Library
Power a constant-power pump when no curve is given
Speed relative speed (1.0 is the speed of the curve); a variable speed drive changes it
Speed profile multipliers of the speed through the day
Efficiency curve, energy tariff, tariff profile for the energy report
Starting status open or closed at the start of the run

A pump that cannot deliver the head the system needs closes for that time step, and the solver warns about it. A pump never lets water flow backwards.

Valves

A valve controls pressure or flow at a point. Each type has its own setting:

Type Setting What it does
Pressure reducing valve (PRV) pressure (m) keeps the pressure after it at the setting; closes if that would reverse the flow, opens fully if the pressure before it is lower
Pressure sustaining valve (PSV) pressure (m) keeps the pressure before it at the setting, for example to protect high ground upstream
Pressure breaker valve (PBV) pressure drop (m) takes away a fixed head, like a break-pressure tank
Flow control valve (FCV) flow (L/s) limits the flow to the setting
Throttle control valve (TCV) loss coefficient K a partly closed valve; 0 is fully open, as an isolation valve
General purpose valve (GPV) a head loss curve any device with its own head loss against flow: filters, meters, backflow preventers

A valve can also be forced open or closed. Valves should not be joined directly to a reservoir or tank, nor two pressure valves set against each other in series: give them a short pipe between.

Data that describe time and behaviour

  • Demand profiles multiply the design withdrawals, period by period (usually hourly over a day). The profile library has typical shapes to start from.
  • Curves: pump head and efficiency against flow, tank volume against depth, head loss against flow for general purpose valves. The library has samples for each type. In an element's properties each curve field lists only the curves of its kind (a tank's Volume curve, a pump's Head-flow curve and Efficiency curve); the button beside it opens the library at that curve, or starts a new one of the right kind, and assigns it when you close the library. A pump's Speed profile and Tariff profile are chosen from the demand profiles.
  • Controls change one pump, pipe or valve at a time or when a level or pressure crosses a value; rules combine several conditions (Controls and rules).
  • Analysis options set the units, the friction formula, the demand model, the solver's limits and the times of an extended period (Analysis options).

The hydraulic model

For each moment the analysis solves two sets of equations together:

  • conservation of mass at every junction: the flows in equal the flows out plus the withdrawal;
  • conservation of energy along every link: the difference of head between its ends equals the head lost in a pipe or valve, or minus the head added by a pump.

Friction

Head lost to friction along a pipe of length L (m), diameter D (m) and flow Q (m3/s):

Formula Head loss Roughness Use it for
Hazen-Williams h = 10.67 L Q^1.852 / (C^1.852 D^4.87) C, larger is smoother water supply in the usual range of velocities
Darcy-Weisbach h = f L V^2 / (2 g D) roughness height e (mm); f from the Colebrook-White law or the laminar law any fluid and flow, the most exact
Chezy-Manning h = 10.29 n^2 L Q^2 / D^5.33 Manning n, smaller is smoother larger mains and open-channel habits

Typical roughness of new pipe:

Material Hazen-Williams C Darcy-Weisbach e (mm) Manning n
PVC, HDPE, PE, PPR 145 to 150 0.0015 to 0.007 0.009
GRP 150 0.01 0.009
Copper 140 0.0015 0.010
Ductile iron, cement lined 130 to 140 0.03 to 0.1 0.011 to 0.012
Steel, cement lined 130 to 140 0.05 0.012
Galvanised iron 120 0.15 0.014
Concrete 120 to 140 0.3 to 3 0.012 to 0.015
Old cast iron 60 to 100 1 to 5 0.015 to 0.017

Roughness grows as pipes age and scale; calibrate it against field pressures and flows.

Minor losses

Fittings add a loss h = K V^2 / (2 g), with K the sum of the loss factors of the bends, tees, valves and meters along the pipe (a gate valve fully open about 0.2, a 90 degree bend about 0.3 to 0.9, a globe valve about 10).

Pumps

A pump adds the head of its curve at the flow through it. A curve given by one point (the design flow and head) is extended to a full curve with shut-off at 133 % of the design head and the largest flow at twice the design flow. A three-point curve (shut-off, design, largest flow) is fitted by a smooth law; a curve with more points is followed point by point. At a relative speed s the curve scales as h(sQ) = s^2 h(Q).

Demand-driven and pressure-driven withdrawal

In a demand-driven analysis every junction receives its full withdrawal whatever the pressure; negative pressures then show where the network cannot deliver it. In a pressure-driven analysis the delivered withdrawal follows the pressure p:

  • nothing below the minimum pressure Pmin;
  • the full withdrawal Q at or above the required pressure Preq;
  • in between, q = Q ((p - Pmin) / (Preq - Pmin))^n, usually with n = 0.5.

Pressure-driven analysis is the one to use for intermittent supply, for networks that cannot meet the peak, and for any study of what customers actually receive.

Emitters

An outflow coefficient C at a junction adds an outflow q = C p^n (n is the emitter exponent of the analysis options, 0.5 for an orifice). Emitters model leaks, sprinklers, fire hydrants and open taps.

The water quality model

The quality analysis follows the substance with the water. Its steps, at each quality time step:

  1. Transport: water moves along each pipe at its velocity, carrying its concentration in segments.

  2. Mixing at junctions: the water leaving a junction has the flow-weighted average of the water arriving, plus any source there.

  3. Mixing in tanks: by the tank's mixing behaviour:

    Behaviour How the tank mixes Typical tank
    Fully mixed all water in the tank mixes at once a tank with one pipe for inlet and outlet
    Two compartments an inlet zone mixes with the inflow, the rest only with the inlet zone a large tank with separate inlet and outlet
    Plug flow, first in first out water leaves in the order it came a tall tank with inlet and outlet at opposite ends
    Stacked, last in first out new water floats on old and leaves first a tank filled and emptied from the bottom

  4. Reactions:

    • in the bulk water, at a rate kb C^n (first order, n = 1: the concentration falls exponentially, halving every ln 2 / kb days);
    • at the pipe wall, at a rate that depends on the wall coefficient kw, the pipe size and how fast the substance can reach the wall (molecular diffusivity);
    • a limiting potential makes a reaction stop at a level (a substance that grows to a ceiling or decays to a floor).

Water age is computed as a substance that grows by one hour every hour; source tracing as the share of water that has passed through the traced node.

Rates by material, bottle tests and the study tools are in Water quality.
