Questions and answers

Answers to the questions engineers ask most often.

Modelling

How do I model a borehole with a submersible pump? A reservoir at the pumping water level (the static level plus the drawdown at the pumped flow), then the pump from that reservoir to a node at the pump's depth, then the riser pipe to the wellhead. The sample Smallholder solar borehole, 1.5 ha drip shows it. The pump's head-flow curve must cover the lift from the pumping level to the delivery point plus the friction.

How do I model a break-pressure tank? As a pressure breaker valve or, more stable in every case, as a pressure reducing valve set to about 1 m: the downstream network then starts from the tank's level. The sample Rural gravity piped scheme uses them.

How do I model a float valve on a tank inlet? Let the tank close its own inlet when full (the default), or add a control: close the inlet pipe when the tank rises above a level, open it below another.

How do I model leakage? With outflow coefficients (emitters) at junctions, whose outflow grows with pressure, or as an extra withdrawal category. Estimate the coefficients from the night flow of a district: the leakage at night pressure gives C = q / p^n.

How do I model intermittent supply? Use a pressure-driven analysis, a demand profile that is zero when the supply is off, and controls that close the main valve during those hours. The delivered withdrawal shows who receives water.

How do I model a hydrant or fire flow? Analysis > Fire Flow Analysis checks every hydrant for the required flow at the residual pressure. For one hydrant in a run, add its flow as a withdrawal at the hydrant junction.

How do I model a pump that runs on solar power only in the day? A clock-time control or a rule: open at 8 AM, closed at 4 PM, and closed when the tank is full. The rule samples include Solar pumping hours with a full-tank stop.

Results

Why are some pressures negative? The network cannot deliver the withdrawals at those junctions with the head available. In a demand-driven run the shortfall shows as negative pressure; run a pressure-driven analysis to see what each junction would actually receive, then enlarge pipes or add head.

Why does my run stop with "unbalanced"? See Errors and warnings: usually a disconnected part, a pump that cannot lift, or valves set against each other.

Why does my tank empty (or fill) every day? Supply and demand over the day do not match: compare the system flow graph (production against consumption), check the pump's controls and the demand profile, and the tank's size.

What is the difference between design withdrawal and delivered withdrawal? The design withdrawal is what the junction asks for; the delivered withdrawal is what the run gave it. They are the same in a demand-driven run; in a pressure-driven run the delivered withdrawal is smaller where the pressure is short.

Design

Which head loss formula should I use? Hazen-Williams is the usual choice for water supply at normal velocities; Darcy-Weisbach is the most exact for any pipe, velocity and temperature. Keep one formula for a project.

What velocities and pressures should I design for? Follow your standard; common values are 0.5 to 1.5 m/s in distribution, a minimum pressure of 10 to 15 m (5 to 10 m at communal taps), and a maximum static pressure of 60 to 80 m. The project's design standard holds the values the tools start from.

How do I choose pipe classes? The pipe class (PN) must exceed the highest pressure the pipe sees, static or in operation, plus an allowance for surge. Check the static pressure with the pumps off and the tanks full; in long rising mains, keep a margin for surge.

Data

Can I use my old models? Yes: FlowCHAOS imports hydraulic model input files (.inp) and binary model files (.net) of the widely used public-domain modelling program; each becomes a network layer, with its profiles, curves, controls and rules. Save your work as a FlowCHAOS project (.fchaos) or network file (.fcnet) to keep everything.

Can I work offline? Yes. Everything works offline except online basemaps, satellite imagery, place search and online terrain. Offline basemaps (MBTiles) and DEMs work without a connection.

Where are my files and settings? Projects are where you save them. Settings, logs and your own reference data are listed in Install, under Where FlowCHAOS keeps things.

The program

Is FlowCHAOS free? Yes. FlowCHAOS is freeware: it is licensed without a fee for commercial, academic and public-sector use under the FlowCHAOS Freeware Licence.

Which Windows do I need? Windows 10 or later, 64-bit, with 4 GB of memory (8 GB for large networks and terrain).

How do I report a problem? Help > Report an Issue, with the project and the value you expected. Calculation questions are answered first.
