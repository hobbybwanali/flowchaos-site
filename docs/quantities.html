User guide

Quantities and planning

Bills of quantities measured from the design and kept with it, and the construction programme that follows them.

The BOQ Workbench

File > Export > Bill of Quantities (or the BOQ button) opens the BOQ Workbench in its own window. The bill is part of the project: it is saved with it, and every change can be undone from the main window.

Generating a bill

Generate asks for the BOQ type (water supply, irrigation, building plumbing), the contents (every section of the type with blanks to complete, or only the work found on the map), the company and client details, the regional preset (currency, tax, levies, contingency), the default pipe materials and the trench sizes. The bill is then measured from the network:

  • pipes by material, outside diameter and pressure class (from the modelled or static pressures);
  • fittings from the geometry: bends by angle, tees, crosses, reducers, end caps, flange adaptors;
  • valves by type and size, chambers, air valves, washouts and hydrants;
  • earthworks from the trench width and depth: clearing, excavation, bedding, backfill and surplus;
  • pumps with their duty point, tanks with their volume and stands, sources and connections.

Editing and tracing

The bill is a tree of bills, elements and items. Double-click a description, unit, quantity, rate or note to change it; add items, elements and bills, move rows up and down, or delete them. The From column tells where each quantity comes from:

From Meaning
Network measured from the map
Lump sum a fixed quantity
To complete a provisional item: type its quantity
Typed added or changed by hand

Select a measured item and press Show on Map: the pipes and nodes it was measured from are selected on the map. When the network changes, Update Quantities measures it again: measured items follow, items typed by hand and every rate are kept, new work is added and work that left the network is marked. Show: Changed by the last update lists what moved.

Pricing

  • Type a rate in the Rate column.
  • Build it up in the Item panel: materials, labour, plant and equipment and transport for one unit, plus overheads and profit; Use This Rate prices the selected items.
  • Rates > Import Rates from a Priced Spreadsheet reads any workbook with Description and Rate columns (a contractor's priced bill, a company rate list) into the project's rate library and prices the matching items. Add This Bill's Rates to the Library keeps your rates for the next bill.
  • Plugins can add their own rate sources (see Writing plugins).

Items with a quantity but no rate are shaded; Show: Quantity but no rate lists them.

Summary

The Summary panel gives each bill's total, the net total, contingency, levies, tax and the grand total, the cost per kilometre of pipe and per connection, how complete the pricing is, and a chart of each bill's share of the cost.

Revisions and export

Revisions > Save a Revision keeps a copy (Rev A, Rev B, ...). Compare with a Revision lists every item whose quantity or rate changed and the change in the grand total; Restore a Revision brings one back. Export writes the bill as a spreadsheet (.xlsx) with the company letterhead, a document (.docx) or CSV.

Fittings as they are made

Fittings follow the pipe material (the materials section of resources/data/boq/catalog.json, which you can edit):

Material Joint Bends made Turns taken without a fitting
Polyethylene up to 90 mm compression 90 degree elbows up to 60 degrees (laid to a curve from the coil)
Polyethylene 110 mm and over butt fusion or electrofusion 11.25, 22.5, 30, 45, 60, 90 up to 11.25 degrees
uPVC, PVC-O rubber ring socket 11.25, 22.5, 45, 90 up to 2 degrees (joint deflection)
Ductile iron push-in socket 11.25, 22.5, 45, 90 up to 4 degrees
GRP double bell coupling 11.25, 22.5, 30, 45, 60, 90 up to 2 degrees
Steel welded cut to the angle up to 3 degrees
GI, PPR, copper threaded, socket fusion, capillary 45, 90 up to 5 degrees

A turn that is not one of the angles made is built from two or three bends (67 degrees in PVC is a 45 and a 22.5). Bends of 11.25 and 22.5 degrees are standard catalogue items for PVC, ductile iron and large polyethylene. The bill also counts:

  • joints between pipe lengths or coils (butt fusion joints every 12 m, compression couplings every coil, threaded sockets, double bell couplings; none for pipes with their own sockets);
  • flange adaptors at valves and pumps as the material needs (stub flange and backing ring for polyethylene, flange adaptor for PVC, flanged spigot for ductile iron);
  • thrust blocks at bends of 22.5 degrees and more, tees and dead ends of 75 mm and larger, but not on welded polyethylene, which carries the end load itself;
  • route marker posts every 100 m, at turns and at valves.

Each pipe takes the lowest pressure class its material is made in that covers the pressure with the margin (polyethylene PN6 to PN25, ductile iron to PN64); a line above the highest class is reported in the notes. The notes also list what to order: the number of lengths or coils of each pipe, with an allowance for waste and cutting.

Construction planning

Analysis > Project Planning opens the planner:

  • a task table and a Gantt chart side by side, with drag to move, resize and link tasks;
  • constraints, deadlines, calendars and working time;
  • critical path, PERT probabilities, resources and levelling;
  • baselines, progress, status date and earned value with the cost curve;
  • schedule risk and a schedule quality check (the Risk and quality tab).

Schedule risk. The plan is scheduled many times (1,000 by default) with each duration drawn at random: from the task's optimistic, most likely and pessimistic days when they are given, otherwise within a range around its duration (90 to 125 % unless you change it). The tab shows the finish date met in half the runs (P50), in 80 (P80) and in 90 (P90), the chance of finishing as planned, the spread of the finish as a chart and how often each task was on the critical path.

Schedule quality check. The 14-point assessment used to judge whether a plan can be relied on: tasks without a predecessor or successor, leads, lags, links other than finish to start, hard constraints, high and negative float, long tasks, invalid progress, tasks without resources, missed tasks, a critical path that runs to the finish, the critical path length index and the baseline execution index. Each row gives what was found, the limit, Pass or Fail and the tasks concerned; checks that need a baseline, a status date or a deadline say so until they have them.

Manual and automatic scheduling. An auto scheduled task takes its dates from its links, constraints and the calendar. Task > Manually Schedule pins the selected tasks to their start dates: they stay there when their predecessors move (the plan warns when a predecessor finishes later), and dragging the bar moves the pinned date. Task > Auto Schedule gives them back to the scheduler. Project > New Tasks Manually Scheduled decides how new tasks start. Manual tasks are drawn with brackets at both ends of the bar and can be listed with View > Filter > Manually Scheduled Tasks.

Formatting the Gantt chart. View > Gantt Chart Style sets the theme (the colours of task, critical, summary and manual bars, milestones, progress, baseline and links, each of which can be changed), the bar shape (rounded, square, shaded or flat) and height, how progress is drawn, the text beside the bars (task names, resource names, durations, dates, percent complete) and the parts shown (links, baseline, critical path, float, non-working days, gridlines, the today and status date lines). View > Gantt Presets applies a whole format at once (Standard, Tracking, Critical path, Presentation, Detailed, Print in black and white); the themes and presets are in resources/data/planning/gantt_styles.json. The style is kept with the plan, and printed and exported charts use it.

View also filters the tasks (critical, milestones, incomplete, late, manually scheduled) and shows the outline to a level. Project > Move Project moves the whole plan to a new start date with every constraint, deadline and manual start. The Resource menu assigns resources to the selected tasks, adds and removes resources, levels them and opens the resource sheet and usage.

The Gantt chart and the network diagram (CPM) each export as PNG, JPEG, TIFF, BMP, SVG or PDF (File > Export). File > New Plan from Template starts from a complete sample, a town water supply scheme with phases, crews and machines, costs, overlapping links, a baseline and progress, or from a template of a smallholder irrigation scheme, a borehole with solar pumping, a pipeline rehabilitation, or a feasibility study and detailed design. A schedule can also be generated from the project's quantities. Plans are saved with the project or as .chaosproj files, and exchanged as project schedule XML.
