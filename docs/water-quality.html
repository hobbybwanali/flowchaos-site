User guide

Water quality

Chlorine residuals, water age, source tracing and contamination events, from set-up to a report.

Analysis > Water Quality Analysis follows a substance or the age of the water through an extended-period run of the network. It is built for the questions a water utility answers every day: does every customer receive enough free chlorine, where does the water stay too long, which works feeds which zone, and what happens if something enters the network.

The window has the study set-up on the left, in the order you fill it, and the results on the right. Everything you set is kept with the project, so the next time the window opens the same study is ready to run again.

1. Start from a ready study

The first box offers ready studies. Each one fills every part of the set-up with this network's own reservoirs, tanks and nodes, so it runs at once and you only change the numbers.

Study What it answers Main settings
Chlorine residual compliance Does every junction keep enough free chlorine? Chlorine 1 mg/L leaving each reservoir, bulk decay -0.55 per day, wall decay by pipe material, limits 0.2 to 5 mg/L, 72 h with 24 h of warm-up
Chlorine with a booster at the service tanks Does re-dosing at the tanks keep the far end above 0.2 mg/L? As above, plus a 0.6 mg/L set-point at every tank
Water age and tank turnover Where is the water older than 72 hours? Water age over 168 h, statistics from hour 96
Where each source's water goes Which zone does each works serve, and where do sources blend? Source tracing from the first reservoir
Contamination event Which nodes does a contaminant reach, when, and how strongly? A mass injection at a central junction from hour 2 to hour 4, 48 h at 15 minute steps, reached above 0.01 mg/L
Fluoride or another conservative chemical Does any junction exceed a maximum? A chemical that does not decay, limit 1.5 mg/L

Custom study keeps whatever you set below. Changing the parameter by hand switches back to a custom study and fills in the usual limits of the new parameter.

2. What to follow

Parameter Unit Meaning
Chlorine (free residual) mg/L A reacting substance: it decays in the water and at the pipe walls
Water age h The time since the water left a source; tanks and dead ends age it
Source tracing % The share of the water at each node that came from the traced source
Chemical mg/L Any substance, conservative (no decay) or reacting

For source tracing, choose the traced source: a reservoir, a tank or any node. The window lists reservoirs and tanks first.

3. Sources, boosters and injections

Each row is one source at one node:

Type Strength means Use it for
Inflow the concentration of water entering the network at a reservoir or tank (mg/L) treatment works, a borehole with its own dosing
Set-point the concentration the node keeps in the water leaving it (mg/L) a booster chlorinator at a tank or a pumping station
Flow-paced the concentration the node adds to the water passing through (mg/L) a dosing pump paced by a flow meter
Mass the mass added each minute (mg/min) a spill, an intrusion, a tracer test

From (h) and To (h) limit a source to part of the run: an accidental intrusion from hour 2 to hour 4, a booster switched on at hour 12, a dosing failure from hour 30. To at 0 means to the end of the run. The source is switched on and off on the time step of the demand profiles (one hour unless you changed it under Analysis Options, Times).

Add adds an empty row, or one booster for every node selected on the map; Remove deletes the selected row. Sources only matter for chlorine and chemicals: water age and tracing need none.

4. Reactions

  • Bulk: the first-order decay rate of the water itself, per day (negative for decay). Free chlorine usually lies between -0.1 and -1.5 per day; warm water and water rich in organic matter decay faster.

  • From a bottle test: fill a dark bottle with water leaving the works, keep it at a steady temperature and read the chlorine twice. Enter the two readings, the hours between them, the bottle temperature and the usual temperature of the water in the network. FlowCHAOS works out the rate, corrects it to the network temperature (the rate changes by a factor of about 1.07 for every degree; you can change the factor) and shows how long chlorine takes to halve.

  • Wall: the rate at which pipe walls consume chlorine, in m/day. One rate for every pipe uses the value below. By pipe material gives each pipe the rate of its material; Rates shows every material class, how many pipes of the network belong to it, and its rate:

    Material Typical wall rate (m/day)
    HDPE / PE, uPVC / PVC -0.01
    GRP -0.02
    Copper -0.05
    Ductile iron (lined), asbestos cement, concrete -0.1
    Steel -0.3
    Galvanised iron -0.4
    Cast iron (unlined) -0.5

    These are starting values to calibrate with field readings. A pipe whose material is not recognised uses the single wall rate; a pipe with its own wall rate in its properties keeps it.

  • Tank decay: the bulk rate inside tanks, the same as in pipes unless you untick As in pipes.

  • Tank mixing: how the water mixes inside the tanks. Complete mixing suits a tank with one pipe for inlet and outlet; Two compartments splits the volume into a zone that mixes with the inflow (its share is the number beside it) and a zone that does not; Plug flow (first in, first out) suits a tall tank with separate inlet and outlet; Stacked (last in, first out) suits a tank where new water floats on old. As each tank is set keeps the model in each tank's properties.

  • Initial value: the value everywhere at the start; tanks start full of it.

5. Limits at junctions

  • Minimum and Maximum: the range every junction must keep, for example free chlorine from 0.2 to 5 mg/L (WHO guidelines), water age at most 72 hours, fluoride at most 1.5 mg/L.
  • Reached above: a junction counts as reached the first time its value rises above this level. The results then give the hour each junction is first reached: the arrival time of a contaminant, or of water from a new source.

6. Time

  • Duration: long enough for tanks and remote pipes to settle; three days for chlorine, a week for water age.
  • Warm-up: the first hours are left out of the statistics while values settle from their starting state.
  • Results every and Quality step: how often results are kept, and the transport step (shorter is more accurate and slower).

Run Water Quality Analysis solves the hydraulics and the transport together with the advanced engine. The window stays usable while it runs.

The results

Summary opens with a coloured verdict: green when every junction stays within the limits, amber when a few leave them, red when many do or when a contaminant reaches junctions. Below it:

  • the number of junctions, the mean, the lowest and the highest value;
  • how many junctions stay within the limits at all times;
  • water delivered within the limits: the share of the water actually delivered to customers (each junction's delivered demand over the time) that met the limits. A dead end that fails but serves few people weighs little; a busy zone that fails weighs a lot. This is the figure a board or a regulator asks for;
  • the junctions that leave the limits, with the share of time they stay within them;
  • for a contamination event, the junctions reached, the first to be reached and the arrival hour at each, and the water delivered at reached junctions;
  • the tanks, and the study set-up, so a printed summary says how it was obtained.

Nodes lists every node with its minimum, mean, maximum and final value, the share of time within the limits, the water delivered after the warm-up and the hour it was first reached. Sort by any column; double-click a row to find the node on the map.

Tanks lists the tanks with their minimum, mean, maximum and final value.

Time Series plots the selected nodes (up to six) over the run, with the limits as dashed lines and the end of the warm-up as a dotted line.

Under the tabs:

  • Show on Map puts the run on the map: Map Styles colours nodes and pipes by concentration, and the time bar and Animate Map play it.
  • Select Failing Junctions selects on the map every junction that leaves the limits.
  • Export CSV writes the node table and the full time series.
  • Report (PDF) writes a printable report: the title, the network, the date, the verdict, the figures, the set-up and the junction table.

A worked example: chlorine in a town network

  1. File > Open Sample Projects > Chlorine residual in a town network.
  2. Analysis > Water Quality Analysis. Choose Chlorine residual compliance.
  3. Click From a bottle test: first reading 1.0 mg/L, second reading 0.6 mg/L after 48 hours, bottle at 25 degrees, network at 22 degrees. The bulk rate becomes about -0.21 per day. Click OK.
  4. Run. The summary shows the lowest residual and where it occurs.
  5. Choose Chlorine with a booster at the service tanks and run again to see what re-dosing at the tanks changes.
  6. Report (PDF) for the file.

A worked example: a contamination event

  1. Select the junction where the contaminant could enter, then choose Contamination event.
  2. In the sources table, set the node, the mass (mg/min) and the hours it lasts.
  3. Run. The verdict gives the number of junctions reached and the first arrival; the Nodes tab, sorted by First reached, gives the order in which to warn customers or close valves.
  4. Show on Map and play the time bar to watch the plume travel.

Tips

  • Run the hydraulics first (Run Extended Period) and fix any warnings: water quality follows the flows, so unbalanced hydraulics give meaningless concentrations.
  • Use a warm-up of at least a day, longer when tanks are large compared with the demand.
  • Calibrate the bulk rate with bottle tests, then the wall rates with chlorine readings at a few hydrants; the Field Observation Set (Analysis menu) holds the readings.
  • The same settings drive every extended-period run when the parameter is set under Analysis Options > Water Quality; the Water Quality Analysis window adds the study tools on top.
